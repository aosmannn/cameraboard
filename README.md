# Wayframe

**Turn your photos into a map of where you've been.**

Wayframe hangs your photos on a world map, right where they were taken. Connect them with a red string into a story of your trip, play it back stop by stop, and share it with friends, or with the world.

**Try it: [cameraboard-xi.vercel.app](https://cameraboard-xi.vercel.app)**

---

## What you can do

- **Pin your photos.** Add pictures and put each one where it was taken. Search a city, click the map, or let a photo's own location place itself.
- **Tell a story.** Click your photos in the order you went and a red string joins them into a route. Reorder it, rename it, and press play to watch the map travel from stop to stop.
- **Make it a poster.** Turn any story into a poster you can print.
- **Share it your way.** Keep everything private, show it to friends, or make it public with a link. Anyone can open a public story, no account needed.
- **See the camera behind the shot.** Wayframe reads the camera, shutter speed, aperture and ISO from each photo and shows them like a caption.
- **Explore.** Browse photos and stories from other people, see where they've been, and find photos by camera.
- **Follow friends.** Find people by username and see their stories on your map next to your own.

## Made for old cameras

Point-and-shoots, old digital cameras, DSLRs and film scans save the date and settings inside every photo, but rarely the place. Wayframe is built for exactly those. Drop a whole day of photos in, put them on the map in a click, and keep the memory of the camera that took them.

## Your photos, your rules

- It works without an account. Your photos stay on your own device until you choose to share them.
- Sign in with just your email, no password, when you want your photos on every device.
- Every photo starts private. You choose Private, Friends, or Public for your whole account, and you can make exceptions for a single photo or story.
- Only people who turn it on are listed in the community gallery.

## About

Hi, I'm Adam. I take pictures on old digital cameras, like a Sony Cyber-shot, and none of them ever knew where they were. My photos were scattered across folders with nothing to connect them. I wanted a place to hang them on the wall, string them together, and show friends where I'd been. So I came up with Wayframe and built it, with my friend Steven helping along the way.

If you shoot on old gear too, I'd love for you to try it.
